# Pennsylvania evolution-curriculum policy history — founding research (2026-10-20)

Full primary-source research on Pennsylvania's K-12 evolution-education policy history, organized in two parts: the Kitzmiller v. Dover Area School District case, and Pennsylvania's separate state-level science standards. Every entry has date, description, named actors, and source. Items confirmed only via a secondary summary rather than a document read directly are flagged explicitly.

## Part 1: Kitzmiller v. Dover Area School District (2005)

### The board's policy

- **June 2004**: Dover Area School Board curriculum committee (chaired by William Buckingham) begins pushing to balance evolution teaching with "creationism" in biology textbook discussions. Buckingham objected that the proposed biology textbook (Kenneth Miller & Joseph Levine's *Biology*) was "laced with Darwinism." Source: trial record / [NCSE, "Design on Trial in Dover, Pennsylvania"](https://ncse.ngo/design-trial-dover-pennsylvania).
- **2004-08-02**: Board votes 5–3 to purchase Miller & Levine's *Biology: The Living Science*, but per trial testimony initially withheld ordering it over the evolution dispute.
- **2004-10-18**: Dover board votes **6–3** to amend the biology curriculum to state: *"Students will be made aware of gaps/problems in Darwin's theory and of other theories of evolution including, but not limited to, intelligent design."* **For**: Alan Bonsell, William Buckingham, Jane Cleaver, Heather Geesey, Sheila Harkins, Angie Ziegler-Yingling. **Against**: Jeffrey Brown, Carol "Casey" Brown, Noel Wenrich. Jeff and Casey Brown (a married couple, board members 5 and 10 years respectively) resigned in protest at the end of that same meeting; Casey Brown's statement: *"it has become increasingly evident that in the direction the board has now chosen to go, holding a certain religious belief is of paramount importance... I can no longer effectively function as a member of this board."* Source: [NCSE, "Kitzmiller v. Dover Timeline"](https://ncse.ngo/kitzmiller-v-dover-timeline) and [NCSE, "Design on Trial in Dover, Pennsylvania"](https://ncse.ngo/design-trial-dover-pennsylvania), summarizing the trial record ([Plaintiffs' Findings of Fact](https://talkorigins.org/faqs/dover/pf.html)).
- **2004-11-19**: District press release explaining implementation (an administrator-read statement, not a teacher-taught unit).
- **All eight Dover High School science teachers** sent the superintendent a letter stating intelligent design "is not science" and "not an accepted scientific theory," asking to be excused from reading the statement since doing so would make them "violate the ethics code of [their] profession." Source: [NCSE, "Dover teachers refuse to read antievolution disclaimer"](https://ncse.com/news/2005/01/dover-teachers-refuse-to-read-antievolution-disclaimer-00664).
- **2005-01-06**: The eight biology teachers formally refused to read the disclaimer. **2005-01-07**: administration agreed administrators would read it instead. First reading scheduled 2005-01-13, delayed (classes hadn't reached the unit; Jan 17 was MLK Day, no school).
- **2005-01-18**: The disclaimer was first read, by the assistant superintendent, in each 9th-grade biology classroom — not by the regular teachers. Roughly 15 students walked out during the reading. Source for the Jan 6/7/13/17/18 sequence: NCSE, "Design on Trial in Dover, Pennsylvania."

**Verbatim disclaimer text** (as reproduced on NCSE's page, matching the trial exhibit):

> "The state standards require students to learn about Darwin's Theory of Evolution and to eventually take a standardized test of which evolution is a part. Because Darwin's Theory is a theory, it is still being tested as new evidence is discovered. The Theory is not a fact. Gaps in the Theory exist for which there is no evidence. A theory is defined as a well-tested explanation that unifies a broad range of observations. Intelligent Design is an explanation of the origin of life that differs from Darwin's view. The reference book, *Of Pandas and People*, is available for students to see if they would like to explore this view in an effort to gain an understanding of what Intelligent Design actually involves. As is true with any theory, students are encouraged to keep an open mind. The school leaves the discussion of the Origins of Life up to individual students and their families. As a standards-driven district, class instruction focuses on the standards and preparing students to be successful on standards-based assessments."

Source: [NCSE, "Design on Trial in Dover, Pennsylvania"](https://ncse.ngo/design-trial-dover-pennsylvania), reproducing the trial exhibit. The exact punctuation is NCSE's transcription rather than a scan of the courtroom exhibit itself, but matches Wikipedia's independent summary of the same document.

### The lawsuit

- **2004-12-14**: Eleven Dover parents filed suit in the US District Court for the Middle District of Pennsylvania. *Kitzmiller et al. v. Dover Area School District et al.*, No. 4:04-cv-02688.
- **Named plaintiffs (11)**: Tammy J. Kitzmiller (lead), Aralene D. Callahan, Frederick B. Callahan, Bryan Rehm, Christy Rehm, Deborah F. Fenimore, Joel A. Leib, Steven Stough, Beth A. Eveland, Cynthia Sneath, Julie Smith. Source: [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/4528445/kitzmiller-v-dover-area-school-district/); [opinion caption, Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/400/707/2414073/).
- **Plaintiffs' legal team**: Pepper Hamilton LLP attorneys Eric Rothschild, Stephen Harvey, Thomas Schmidt, Alfred Wilcox (pro bono, lead trial counsel); Richard Katskee and Alex Luchenitser of Americans United for Separation of Church and State; Vic (Witold) Walczak and Paula Knudsen of the ACLU of Pennsylvania. Source: [ACLU, "The Trial of Kitzmiller v. Dover"](https://www.aclu.org/documents/trial-kitzmiller-v-dover); [ACLU of PA case page](https://www.aclupa.org/cases/kitzmiller-v-dover/).
- **Defense counsel**: Thomas More Law Center attorneys Richard Thompson, Patrick Gillen, Robert Muise. (The full attorney roster/"lead" framing is secondary-sourced; the names recur consistently across sources.)
- **Discovery Institute's relationship to the case**: repeatedly tried to distance itself from Dover's specific policy. Its own site states it "repeatedly advised the Dover School Board and [Thomas More Law Center] that the board's intelligent design policy... was problematic and should be replaced," that the board and TMLC "chose to reject [that] advice," and that "misguided policies like the one adopted by the Dover School District are likely to be politically divisive." Three affiliated fellows who had agreed to serve as defense experts — William Dembski, Stephen Meyer, John Angus Campbell — withdrew before depositions, reportedly over a dispute with Thomas More Law Center about each expert needing independent counsel at deposition. Source: [Discovery Institute, "Setting the Record Straight about Discovery Institute's Role in the Dover School District Case"](https://www.discovery.org/a/3003/) (the organization's own published position); the withdrawal detail is corroborated by Wikipedia, not independently confirmed via a Discovery Institute primary document.

### The trial

- **Judge**: John E. Jones III, US District Court, Middle District of Pennsylvania (a George W. Bush appointee — later-noted detail since the ruling cut against the administration's own political base).
- **Trial dates**: 2005-09-26 through 2005-11-04 (closing arguments), Harrisburg, PA — six weeks of testimony, sometimes called "Scopes 2" or the "Dover trial." Source: [NPR, "Pa. Case Weighs Intelligent Design in Schools"](https://www.npr.org/2005/09/26/4864027/pa-case-weighs-intelligent-design-in-schools) (Sept 26, 2005); [NCSE's trial transcript index](https://ncse.ngo/kitzmiller-trial-transcripts).
- **Plaintiffs' expert witnesses**: Kenneth R. Miller (Brown University cell biologist, lead science witness — testified ID including the bacterial-flagellum "irreducible complexity" argument is not testable science, citing peer-reviewed research on flagellum evolutionary precursors); Barbara Forrest (philosopher, SE Louisiana University — testified on ID's documented history as relabeled creationism, using draft manuscripts of *Of Pandas and People* showing "cdesign proponentsists," a literal leftover word-fragment from a find-and-replace of "creationists" to "design proponents" made right after *Edwards v. Aguillard* (1987) banned "creation science"); Kevin Padian (paleontologist, UC Berkeley); Robert Pennock (philosopher of science, Michigan State); John Haught (theologian, Georgetown); Brian Alters (science-education researcher).
- **Defense expert witnesses**: Michael Behe (Lehigh University biochemist, author of *Darwin's Black Box*) and Scott Minnich (University of Idaho microbiologist) — the two who stayed through trial after Dembski/Meyer/Campbell withdrew. Behe's testimony centered on the bacterial flagellum as "irreducible complexity." Under cross-examination, Behe conceded there are "no peer reviewed articles by anyone... providing detailed, rigorous accounts of how intelligent design of any biological system occurred," and that his own definition of "theory" was broad enough to include astrology. Sources: [TalkOrigins trial-transcript archive, Day 1 AM](https://talkorigins.org/faqs/dover/day1am.html) (Miller), [Day 11 PM](https://talkorigins.org/faqs/dover/day11pm.html) (Behe).

### The November 8, 2005 school board election

All **eight** Republican incumbents who supported the ID policy were defeated by the "Dover CARES" slate: Bernadette Reinking, Terry Emig, Bryan Rehm (also a named plaintiff), Herbert "Rob" McIlvaine, Judy McIlvaine, Larry Gurreri, Patricia Dapp, Phil Herman. Ousted incumbents included board president Sheila Harkins and former board president Alan Bonsell. Source: [NCSE, "Dover voters choose good science at polls"](https://ncse.ngo/dover-voters-choose-good-science-polls). The new board's stated position was that it would drop the policy regardless of the pending ruling, and did. Source: [NBC News, "Dover voters oust intelligent design supporters"](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna9973228) (Nov 9, 2005).

### The December 20, 2005 ruling

Judge Jones issued a **139-page opinion**, *Kitzmiller v. Dover Area School District*, **400 F. Supp. 2d 707 (M.D. Pa. 2005)**. Full text: [NCSE-hosted PDF](https://ncse.ngo/files/pub/legal/kitzmiller/highlights/2005-12-20_Kitzmiller_decision.pdf); [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/400/707/2414073/).

Key findings: **Intelligent design is not science** (it invokes a supernatural designer, "a hallmark of a religious, rather than scientific, position"). **ID is a form of creationism, relabeled** — the *Of Pandas and People* "cdesign proponentsists" artifact was especially persuasive evidence that ID's proponents substituted "intelligent design" for "creationism" after *Edwards v. Aguillard* (1987) barred "creation science." Both sides agreed the **Lemon v. Kurtzman** (1971) test applied; Jones also applied the **endorsement test**, finding the board acted with religious intent and the policy conveyed "a strong official endorsement of religion," violating the Establishment Clause (and the parallel Pennsylvania constitutional provision).

**"Breathtaking inanity" quote** (exact sentence): *"The breathtaking inanity of the Board's decision is evident when considered against the factual backdrop which has now been fully revealed through this trial."* Refers to the board's decision to adopt the ID policy, once the full trial record (including contradictory member testimony about motives) had been laid out. Jones also found Buckingham and Bonsell gave false testimony denying the policy's religious origins, writing they lied to "avoid the obvious conclusion that religious convictions played... a significant role" in the decision. Source: the opinion itself; independently corroborated by [*TIME*, "'Breathtaking Inanity': How Intelligent Design Flunked Its Test Case"](https://time.com/archive/6931851/breathtaking-inanity-how-intelligent-design-flunked-its-test-case/) (2005).

**Not appealed.** On **2006-01-03**, the newly elected board voted unanimously (voice vote, no discussion) to formally rescind the ID policy. New board president Bernadette Reinking: *"This is it."* Sources: [NBC News, "Intelligent-design policy rescinded"](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna10698535); [Boston Globe/AP, "Pa. board rescinds school policy"](http://archive.boston.com/news/nation/articles/2006/01/04/pa_board_rescinds_school_policy/) (Jan 4, 2006).

### Aftermath and legacy

**Legal fees**: On **2006-02-21**, the Dover board voted to pay **$1,000,011** in plaintiffs' attorneys' fees and costs (the $11 = $1 nominal damages to each of 11 plaintiffs, plus $1,000,000 toward fees/costs). Plaintiffs' actual documented fees/expenses totaled $2,067,226; Pepper Hamilton (pro bono) billed only its ~$267,000 in hard costs, the remainder split between Americans United and ACLU of PA as cost reimbursement. Source: [NCSE, "'Intelligent design' costs Dover over $1,000,000"](https://ncse.ngo/intelligent-design-costs-dover-over-1000000).

NCSE and the science-education community treated the ruling as a landmark precedent — the first federal test of intelligent design specifically (as opposed to "creation science" generically, which *Edwards v. Aguillard* already addressed in 1987), framed as having "effectively stopped other public schools nationwide from trying to inject [ID] into the science curriculum," cited as persuasive precedent in later cases (e.g. *Maine v. ACLU*, 2011). This framing is a secondary synthesis, not one single retrospective document read end-to-end — the underlying facts are well-corroborated.

**Pennsylvania state-level legislative response**: **House Bill 1007** (2005 session) would have let local school boards add "intelligent design" to any curriculum including evolution and let teachers introduce "supporting evidence" for ID. Got a hearing in the House Subcommittee on Basic Education in **June 2005** (while the Dover trial was underway) but died in committee. Prime sponsor not independently confirmed via primary legislative records (PA's palegis.us has no accessible 2005-session archive entry); existence/content corroborated via NCSE's "Evolution in Pennsylvania" page and Wikipedia's "Anti-evolution legislation" article. Separately, **House Resolution 177** (Rep. Gibson Armstrong) created a PA Select Committee on Academic Freedom in **Higher Education** (not K-12) — a general campus-speech effort connected to the national David Horowitz-style campaign, not an evolution/ID bill. A **2013** Pennsylvania Family Institute draft bill letting teachers present "alternative theories" of evolution/climate change "without facing sanctions" was reported in development but, per NCSE, never actually introduced as a numbered bill. **No PA state "academic freedom act" targeting K-12 evolution instruction has ever passed**, before or after Dover.

## Part 2: Pennsylvania's state-level science standards

### The actual standards document and PA's standards-setting process

**Official title (pre-2022)**: "Academic Standards for Science and Technology," codified as **22 Pa. Code, Chapter 4, Appendix B**, title page reading "Final Form — January 5, 2002" — confirming **adoption/publication date 2002-01-05**. Per NCSE, this was the **first PA science standards document to mention evolution explicitly at all**. This is the version in effect during the 2004-2005 Dover controversy; Dover's own disclaimer explicitly invoked "the state standards" as the reason evolution had to be taught.

**Evolution content**: organized under heading **3.3 Biological Sciences**, subsection **D. Evolution**; grade-12 benchmark **Standard 3.3.10.D** requires students to "analyze the theory of evolution." (Full verbatim text of 3.3.10.D/3.3.12.D not independently re-confirmed this session beyond this characterization — the PDF fetched directly was a 2009 draft elementary-standards document, not the 2002 high-school text.)

**A real pre-Dover PA state-level evolution controversy (2000-2002)**: during the 2002 standards' rulemaking, the **proposed** Standard 3.3.10.D required students to analyze records/studies "**that support or do not support the theory of evolution**." Per the **Pennsylvania Bulletin**'s own final-rulemaking preamble (Vol. 32, Issue 1, Document No. 02-9, [pacodeandbulletin.gov](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pabull?file=/secure/pabulletin/data/vol32/32-1/9.html), read directly), **over 120 individuals and organizations** submitted public comments objecting that the wording "inappropriately singled out" evolution and could "permit or even encourage schools to teach creationism or intelligent design." The **Independent Regulatory Review Commission (IRRC)** also raised concern. The **State Board changed the final-form language to "relevant to the theory of evolution"** and stated explicitly it "did not intend to encourage the teaching of creationism or intelligent design" and the standard was "written to encourage critical thinking." Pennsylvania had its own real, document-level fight over evolution-standard wording nearly five years before Dover reached federal court.

**How PA's standards-setting process works**: the **State Board of Education** proposes/adopts standards by regulation under the Commonwealth's Regulatory Review Act. Proposed rulemaking goes to the **Independent Regulatory Review Commission (IRRC)** and the **chairs of the House and Senate Education Committees**; IRRC can object within a review window, and both committees can hold public hearings (for the 2022 STEELS revision, each held two days of hearings with 40+ commenters, plus 44 formal written comment letters). A materially different, multi-body, longer process than a single board's up-or-down vote — at least three separate institutional-review layers (State Board → IRRC → legislative committees) before legal effect via Pennsylvania Bulletin publication.

### Recent (2010s-2020s) revisions

Pennsylvania's science standards went **20 years without revision** (2002-2022) — the oldest in the country by replacement time, per [WHYY](https://whyy.org/articles/pa-hasnt-updated-science-education-standards-since-2002-new-plan-would-be-first-to-acknowledge-climate-change/). Formal review launched **2019**, developed with input from "over 900 stakeholders," producing the **STEELS standards** (Science, Technology & Engineering, Environmental Literacy & Sustainability) — the name itself signals environmental/sustainability content was the main addition, not evolution content.

**Adoption timeline**: State Board approval **January 2022**; amendments to 22 Pa. Code Chapter 4 published as final-form regulation in the Pennsylvania Bulletin **2022-07-16**; IRRC approval reportedly ~2022-05-19 (secondary source, not independently verified, consistent with the normal sequence). **Full classroom implementation mandatory starting the 2025-2026 school year** (prior standards sunset **2025-06-30**) after a three-year transition.

**Evolution was not the controversy.** No evidence found of public controversy specifically about evolution content during the STEELS revision (2019-2022). NCSE gave PA's old (2002) standards an **"A" for high-school evolution content** (Mead & Mates 2009, *Evolution: Education and Outreach*) and an **"A" for middle-school evolution** (Vazquez 2017, same journal) — evolution was never the weak point. By contrast, the 2002 standards got an **"F" for climate change** in NCSE's 2020 "Making the Grade?" report ([PDF](https://ncse.ngo/files/MakingTheGrade_Final_10.8.2020.pdf)), and that grade drove public comment during 2019-2022 (e.g. climate scientist Michael Mann publicly critiqued a draft version's climate treatment, June 2021 — [NCSE](https://ncse.ngo/concern-about-climate-change-pennsylvanias-proposed-science-standards), checked directly, no evolution angle found). **No PA evolution controversy comparable to Florida's 2026 rewrite fight was found for 2023-2026** — if one is happening, it hasn't surfaced in national/NCSE coverage as of this research pass (Oct 2026).

### Public-records/copyright stance

Pennsylvania does **not** follow the federal public-domain-by-default model. **71 Pa. Stat. Ann. § 636** affirmatively authorizes the Commonwealth to claim copyright on its own publications (discretionary, requiring an affirmative choice by the Secretary of Property and Supplies with Governor approval) — a materially different legal posture than Florida's. Source: [Harvard Library copyright-by-state guide](https://copyright.lib.harvard.edu/states/pennsylvania/); [FindLaw statute page](https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-71-ps-state-government/pa-st-sect-71-636/) (secondary restatements of a real, checkable statute). **In practice**: both the 2002 standards PDF and a 2009 draft elementary-standards PDF, inspected directly, show **no copyright notice, ©, or rights-reservation statement anywhere** — the 2009 draft explicitly states "These standards are offered as a voluntary resource for Pennsylvania's schools." So while PA law permits the Commonwealth to assert copyright, these specific documents do not exercise that option.
