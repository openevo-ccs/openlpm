# national_ccs_policy

Federal/multi-state actors relevant to Florida's evolution-curriculum
politics but not themselves Florida institutions — the national advocacy
organizations, think tanks, and professional/scientific bodies named
repeatedly across both the 2008 NGSSS fight and the 2026 proposed-standards
fight. Equivalent in spirit to `nys_lp`'s/`deutsche_lp`'s national-level
folder, but scoped here to the specific national actors this repo's research
actually surfaced rather than a general US-education-policy survey.

## What's here

[`records/institutional-actors.yaml`](records/institutional-actors.yaml) — 5
actor records: the National Center for Science Education (NCSE, the single
most consistent institutional voice across both episodes and the direct
source of the article that prompted this repo's founding), the Discovery
Institute (authored the model "Academic Freedom Act" legislation Florida's
2008 bill followed), the Thomas B. Fordham Institute (the national
standards-grading body that gave Florida's pre-2008 standards an "F"),
the National Academy of Sciences (endorsed the original 2008 NGSSS draft),
and Answers in Genesis (recorded specifically to document that its role in
Florida's history was commentary-only, not direct involvement — an absence
worth stating precisely rather than leaving ambiguous).

No `policy-timeline-event`, `institutional-mandate-record`, or
`policy-controversy-record` entries live at the national level in this
repo's founding session — Florida's evolution-curriculum history, unlike
Germany's KMK-coordinated 16-Land system, has no equivalent national
coordinating mandate; every substantive event is state-level (see
`state/florida/`). This folder may grow if a future session adds a federal
court ruling (Epperson v. Arkansas, Edwards v. Aguillard) directly relevant
to Florida's own legal posture, which this founding session did not
research.
