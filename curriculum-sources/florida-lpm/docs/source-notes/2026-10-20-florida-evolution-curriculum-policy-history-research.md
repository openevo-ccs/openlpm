## 1. 1990s Sunshine State Standards (original Florida science standards)

**~1993–1996: Development and adoption.** Development began 1993; the Florida Board of Education gave initial adoption in May 1996. Standards covered eight subjects, broken into "benchmarks." Source: [Wikipedia, "Sunshine State Standards"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_State_Standards) (carries a May 2025 staleness notice; does not cover the evolution controversy — bare adoption timeline only).

**1996/1999 standards and the word "evolution."** Florida's original science standards reportedly did not use the term "evolution." A 2005 statement attributed to then-Education Commissioner John Winn says the standards referred only to "biological change over time," which Winn called "more accurate and acceptable," while the Grades 9–12 standards did include evolution-adjacent concepts (natural selection, mutation) without naming the theory itself.

**CONFIDENCE FLAG:** The Winn quote and "changes over time" wording came through AI-synthesized search summaries of secondary sources, not a document read directly. No working link to an FLDOE archival copy of the 1996/1999 text was found. Needs a direct-document check before treating as verbatim.

**2005: Fordham Foundation grades Florida's standards "F."** The Thomas B. Fordham Foundation's *The State of State Science Standards 2005* (lead author Paul R. Gross) gave Florida's then-current standards (described in secondary sources as "the 1999 version" — whether "1996" and "1999" are the same document or two revisions is unconfirmed) a failing grade: "The E-word is sedulously avoided"; "The superficiality of the treatment of evolutionary biology alone justifies the grade 'F.'" Evolution scored 0; Florida wasn't singling out evolution — plate tectonics, cell theory, atomic theory, electromagnetism, and the Big Bang got the same vague treatment. Source: referenced in [NCSE, "Evolution Comes to Florida's Science Standards"](https://ncse.ngo/evolution-comes-floridas-science-standards) and [NCSE, "Evolution in the Florida state science standards" (Oct 23, 2007)](https://ncse.ngo/evolution-florida-state-science-standards). The Fordham PDF itself was not independently fetched — pull directly from edexcellence.net before treating the exact wording as final. Earlier Fordham reviews (1998, 2000) of Florida are referenced but their content/grades were not confirmed.

## 2. The 2008 Next Generation Sunshine State Standards (NGSSS) fight

The best-documented episode — verified across NCSE's contemporaneous articles, which themselves cite Orlando Sentinel, Miami Herald, AP, Education Week, St. Petersburg Times, Tallahassee Democrat, Lakeland Ledger, and Ft. Myers News-Press by date.

**2007-10-19: FLDOE releases draft revised standards for public comment.** A 60-day public comment window (hosted at tools.fcit.usf.edu/ScienceReview/) opened on a draft — the first explicit, named use of "evolution" in Florida's standards. Adoption expected January 2008 (slipped to February). Source: [NCSE, Oct 23 2007](https://ncse.ngo/evolution-florida-state-science-standards) (cites Orlando Sentinel Oct 20 2007, Florida Today/Tallahassee Democrat editorials Oct 23 2007, South Florida Sun-Sentinel editorial Oct 24 2007).

**Late 2007: Organized opposition.** Focus on the Family joined in November 2007; the Florida Baptist Witness ran pieces against the draft (Nov 30, 2007); the Florida Family Policy Council (John Stemberger) organized opposition. At least 12 Florida county school boards passed resolutions urging revision so evolution would not be "presented as fact" — named counties: Baker County (5–0), Polk County, Taylor County (Superintendent Oscar Howard named; Polk board member Kay Fields named in opposition). Source: [NCSE, "Evolution Comes to Florida's Science Standards"](https://ncse.ngo/evolution-comes-floridas-science-standards) (cites Lakeland Ledger Nov 13 & 20 2007, St. Petersburg Times Dec 6 2007 & Dec 25 2005, Florida Baptist Witness Nov 30 2007).

**Writing/framing committee (named):** Gerry Meisels (USF chemistry professor), Debra Walker (archaeologist, Monroe County school board member), Fred Cutting (retired aerospace engineer).

**Pro-science counter-organizing: Florida Citizens for Science (FCS).** A statewide 501(c)(3) founded 2006; members directly involved in framing/writing the draft; ran a petition with 1,700+ signatures. Named figures: Brandon Haught (co-founder, Communications Director, environmental-science teacher, University High School, Volusia County; later author of *Going Ape: Florida's Battles over Evolution in the Classroom*, Univ. Press of Florida, 2014), Joe Wolf (FCS president), Jonathan Smith. Sources: [NCSE, "The e-word arrives in Florida"](https://ncse.ngo/e-word-arrives-florida); [NCSE, "Evolution Comes to Florida's Science Standards"](https://ncse.ngo/evolution-comes-floridas-science-standards); flascience.org.

**Scientific community support:** National Academy of Sciences sent a letter endorsing the original draft (cited by board member Roberto Martinez at the Feb 19 2008 vote); American Institute for Biological Sciences, Florida Academy of Sciences, Americans United for Separation of Church and State, and the Clergy Letter Project also supported it. Named supporters: Joseph Travis (Dean, Arts & Sciences, FSU), Sir Harold Kroto (Nobel laureate).

**2008-02-19: State Board of Education votes 4–3 to adopt, with a late compromise.** Days before the vote, under pressure, Commissioner Eric Smith redrafted the standards to insert "the scientific theory of" before "evolution" (and before plate tectonics, cell theory, atomic theory, electromagnetism, gravity/"scientific law of"). Board member Donna Callaway's push for an additional "critical analysis" amendment did NOT pass.

Named vote (4–3): **YES** (adopted the "scientific theory of" compromise, "Option B") — T. Willard Fair (Chairman), Linda Taylor, Kathleen Shanahan, Phoebe Raulerson. **NO** — Roberto Martinez (defended the original unqualified draft as scientifically stronger — his "no" was in defense of evolution), Akshay Desai, Donna Callaway (opposed from the other direction, wanting weaker language).

Quotes: Martinez (AP, Feb 19 2008): "What we have here is an effort by people to water down our standards." Meisels (AP, Feb 11 2008): "We are frustrated by the disproportionate publicity and the political pressure that has been brought to bear." Walker (Orlando Sentinel): "Do we want these boards setting science policy in Florida? I think not." Haught (Education Week, Feb 19 2008): "The standards, as approved, are a huge step forward for our Florida schools." Gross (Tallahassee Democrat, Feb 25 2008) called the late redraft "transparent and wacky."

Sources: Orlando Sentinel (Oct 27 2007; Feb 12/16/17/19/23 2008); Miami Herald (Feb 17/19 2008); AP (Feb 11/19 2008); Education Week (Nov 7 2007; [Feb 19 2008](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/fla-panels-evolution-vote-hailed/2008/02)); St. Petersburg Times (Feb 12 2008); Lakeland Ledger (Feb 12 2008); Ft. Myers News-Press (Feb 16 2008); Tallahassee Democrat (Feb 25 2008); Florida Baptist Witness (Feb 21 2008); Wired News (Dec 10 2007); compiled via NCSE's two articles above; [*Science*, "Florida Standards Support Evolution—With a Twist"](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.319.5867.1168); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna23238493); [Answers in Genesis's reaction (Feb 23 2008)](https://answersingenesis.org/public-school/science-standards/florida-now-supports-teaching-evolution/) — AiG did not claim direct involvement, purely reactive commentary quoting NCSE's Eugenie Scott.

**Discovery Institute involvement:** confirmed via the subsequent Academic Freedom Act push (below), not the standards vote itself. No Answers in Genesis involvement in shaping the standards or legislation was found — AiG's role was commentary only.

**Post-2008 Fordham re-grade:** Fordham's 2012 50-state review regraded the (by-then NGSSS) standards — one source situates it at a D initially, later revised to C (unconfirmed against a primary Fordham document; surfaced via an AI-synthesized summary of FCS's own timeline page). Lawrence S. Lerner (Fordham co-author) is separately on record reviewing an earlier 2008 draft favorably. Source: [Education Week, "Many States Receive 'D' or 'F'..." (2012)](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/many-states-receive-d-or-f-in-review-of-science-standards/2012/02).

## 3. "Academic Freedom Act" bills and the 2017 instructional-materials law

**2005: HB 837, "Academic Freedom Bill of Rights"** (Rep. Dennis Baxley, R-Ocala) — modeled on David Horowitz's "Academic Bill of Rights" (NOT the Discovery Institute template; unrelated to the evolution-specific push). Targeted university-level viewpoint diversity; would have let students sue professors. Died on the calendar May 2005; not reintroduced 2006. Source: [Wikipedia, "Florida House Bill H-837"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_House_Bill_H-837) (cites Nature, April 2005; Florida Senate bill records; Inside Higher Ed; St. Petersburg Times; Democracy Now!).

**2008: SB 2692 / HB 1483, the real "Academic Freedom Act" fight, tied to Discovery Institute's model legislation.** Sen. Ronda Storms (R-Valrico) introduced SB 2692 following Discovery Institute's model statute at academicfreedompetition.com — the same template later enacted in Louisiana (June 2008) and Tennessee (2012). Rep. Alan Hays (House District 11) introduced the House companion HB 1483, later narrowed to language calling for "thorough presentation and critical analysis of the scientific theory of evolution." John Stemberger (Florida Family Policy Council) was lead advocate, citing anecdotal/unnamed claims of teacher reprisals. Hays hosted a closed March 2008 press event with a Discovery Institute representative and an unnamed film's production team, screened for legislators only.

**Outcome:** House passed its substituted version (HB 1483 text swapped into SB 2692) 71–43 on April 28, 2008. Senate rejected the substitution, restored its own text, sent it back — House left it untouched; it died at session's end without becoming law.

Sources: [Discovery Institute, "Anti-Freedom Activists Try to Censor Science Education in Florida"](https://www.discovery.org/a/4510/); [Evolution News, "Prepared Remarks for Florida Academic Freedom Bill Press Conference" (March 2008)](https://evolutionnews.org/2008/03/prepared_remarks_for_florida_a/); FCS news releases ([1](https://www.flascience.org/wp/?p=529), [2](https://www.flascience.org/?p=534), [3](https://www.flascience.org/?p=547)); [Florida Senate bill-amendment record](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2008/2692/Amendment/307351/PDF); [Panda's Thumb](https://pandasthumb.org/archives/2008/03/explore-the-str.html). **CONFIDENCE FLAG:** the Tampa Tribune's April 29 2008 account of the House/Senate standoff came via search synthesis, not direct fetch.

**2017: HB 989, the instructional-materials-objection law.** CS/CS/HB 989, "Instructional Materials" (Rep. Byron Donalds, R-Naples, later a U.S. Congressman; 20 co-introducers) overhauled textbook review/adoption and let **any Florida resident** (not just parents) formally challenge a district's instructional materials, triggering a hearing before an independent review officer. Introduced March 7, 2017; passed House 94–25 (April 20, 2017); passed Senate 19–17 (May 5, 2017; companion SB 1210 tabled); signed by Gov. Rick Scott June 26, 2017; effective July 1, 2017; Chapter law 2017-177. Source: [Florida Senate bill page](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2017/00989).

**Use against evolution content, 2017–2018:**
- **Nassau County (Nov 2017):** first district challenged. A resident targeted evolution's "prominent place" in three textbooks, stating a goal "to stop the teaching of Darwinian evolution as fact," requesting disclaimer stickers. An independent hearing officer reviewed it; the school board voted down the sticker proposal, reportedly over First Amendment litigation risk.
- **Collier County and Martin County (2018):** separate evolution/climate-change textbook complaints both failed 3–2 at the board level; original textbooks adopted as written.
- At least 7 complaints statewide in the first 5 months after the law took effect, including 2+ specifically targeting evolution and climate content.

Sources: [The World/PRX (Sept 10 2017)](https://theworld.org/stories/2017/09/10/florida-allows-any-resident-challenge-textbooks-and-it-has-some-science-supporters-concerned); [FCS, "Nassau County textbook challenge"](https://www.flascience.org/?p=2997); [FCS running timeline](https://www.flascience.org/?p=3506); [NCSE, "Antiscience bills in Florida" (Feb 2017)](https://ncse.com/news/2017/02/antiscience-bills-florida-0018469); [Columbia Sabin Center](https://climate.law.columbia.edu/content/new-teaching-materials-law-proposed-florida).

**Related, same era:** Florida Student and School Personnel Religious Liberties Act (2017; Sen. Dennis Baxley, Rep. Kim Daniels) allows religiously-based answers on assignments — flagged by FCS as a parallel mechanism, though a separate law from HB 989.

**Subsequent bills (did not pass):** Sen. Dennis Baxley filed bills in 2018 and 2019 requiring "controversial theories and concepts" (evolution/climate change, unnamed but clearly targeted) be taught "factually, objectively, and in a balanced manner" — both died in committee. Source: [FCS's own timeline](https://www.flascience.org/?p=3506) (first-party advocacy source, generally reliable on dates/outcomes, explicitly pro-evolution-education viewpoint).

## 4. The 2019–2020 "B.E.S.T. Standards" rewrite — an important correction

Gov. DeSantis issued Executive Order 19-32 (Jan 31, 2019) to eliminate Common Core. The State Board formally adopted the new **B.E.S.T. (Benchmarks for Excellent Student Thinking) Standards on February 12, 2020** (one secondary source says "January 12, 2020" — unresolved one-month discrepancy). [DeSantis announcement](https://www.flgov.com/eog/news/press/2020/governor-ron-desantis-announces-floridas-new-best-k-12-standards).

**The correction: B.E.S.T. Standards cover English Language Arts and Mathematics only.** Science (and Social Studies) were explicitly NOT rewritten in 2019–2020 and continued under the existing 2008 NGSSS the entire time. Confirmed by Florida's own [B.E.S.T. Standards Overview (FLDOE, July 20, 2020)](https://www.fldoe.org/file/7576/BESTStandardsOverview.pdf) and secondary teacher-resource summaries. Fordham's own 2020 review (*The State of the Sunshine State Standards: Florida's B.E.S.T. Edition*, June 2020, [ERIC ED606003](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED606003)) graded only ELA and Math (both "weak") — no science content, because there was none to review.

**Chronology implication:** no separate "B.E.S.T. Science Standards" rewrite happened in 2019–2020, and no evolution-specific controversy at that time distinct from the ongoing HB 989 fights and the 2024 climate-content removal (below). The 2008 NGSSS biology benchmarks — including **SC.912.L.15.1** ("Explain how the scientific theory of evolution is supported by the fossil record, comparative anatomy, comparative embryology, biogeography, molecular biology, and observed evolutionary change") and the broader standard **SC.912.L.15, "Diversity and Evolution of Living Organisms"** (evolution as "the fundamental concept underlying all of biology," "supported by multiple forms of scientific evidence") — remained in force essentially unchanged from 2008 until the 2026 rewrite. Multiple 2026 sources explicitly describe the current rewrite as the first since 2008, confirming this 18-year gap. Benchmark text aggregated via CPALMS-derived teacher-resource sites (study.com, JoVE, commonplanner.com) reproducing FLDOE's language — **CPALMS.org itself was not fetched directly this session; pull the authoritative text from there in a follow-up.**

**2024: Climate-change content quietly stripped from textbooks under the existing NGSSS regime.** Summer 2024, FLDOE asked publishers submitting science textbooks for state adoption to reduce/remove climate-change content; publishers complied. Teachers described a resulting "culture of caution." Not a formal standards rewrite — operated through the textbook-adoption review process (the same process HB 989 opened to resident challenges in 2017). Source: [WUSF/Hechinger Report joint investigation, republished by NCSE](https://ncse.ngo/how-florida-quietly-removed-climate-change-content-textbooks) (Aug 20 2026, reporting on 2024 events). Named: Glenn Branch (NCSE), Kenneth R. Miller (textbook co-author; Chair, NCSE Board).

## 5. The 2026 proposed new standards — the actual first science-standards rewrite since 2008

**What's proposed:** full K-12 science standards rewrite, first since 2008, released for public comment. Reporting converges on:
- The word **"evolution" does not appear** in the HS life-science standards at all; evolution-adjacent content exists scattered across units without the explicit framing. MS standards reference natural selection without naming evolution.
- Named missing vocabulary (per reviewers at comment sessions): gene flow, genetic drift, origins of life, evidence of evolution, DNA transcription, genetics.
- **"Climate change" and "global warming" do not appear anywhere** in the draft (which retains "weather and climate," "climate systems," "climate impacts on biodiversity"). The 2008 standards explicitly required human-caused climate-change content in several HS electives (Earth science, marine science, environmental science) — that explicit requirement is gone.
- The draft is not built on the National Research Council's 2012 *Framework for K-12 Science Education* (the NGSS-aligned framework most states now use).
- Roughly **1,000 HS science benchmarks**; Glenn Branch (NCSE): "a mile wide and an inch deep," much of it "fun-fact-style trivia"; echoed by former teacher Mindy Pearson, who called it an "encyclopedia of scientific facts."

**Process:** Review began November 2024 (FLDOE). Writing committee: 18 people, only 1 a science-education professor, rest school-district employees; FLDOE reportedly has no designated K-12 science specialists on staff currently (per former specialists Patricia Duncan and Alicia Foy, and Duncan's current on-record comments). Draft released for public comment Sept 25, 2026 (one source says Sept 27 — minor discrepancy, Sept 25 2026 was a Friday, treated here as the correct date). Listening tour: first in-person session Sept 29/30, 2026, Armwood HS, Seffner (Hillsborough County); virtual statewide session Oct 8, 2026 (~300 participants); further sessions at Walton HS (DeFuniak Springs) and Orange County (Lake Buena Vista HS, Oct 27 2026, 5:30–7:30pm). Public comment: fldoe.org/standardsreview/. Implementation if adopted: phased from school year 2028–29 (K–2) and 2029–30 (older grades); teacher training from Spring 2027. **State Board vote expected December 2026.**

**Named FLDOE officials defending the draft:** **Henry Mack** (Education Commissioner): "We want evolutionary biology in the standards, they are in the standards... My kids are reading Charles Darwin"; "We are teaching climate science... Sea level rise is in the standards." Separately (WUSF): "Science begins in wonder, not in ideology. The classical tradition taught that nature is intelligible because it is ordered — that the book of creation can be read by reason. Florida's new standards will form students who observe carefully, reason rigorously, and treat the natural world as something given to be known, not invented." (Worth flagging on its own — signals an explicit ideological/pedagogical framing behind the rewrite.) **Paul O. Burns** (FLDOE Chancellor) also defended the draft (exact quote not captured). **Patricia Duncan** (FLDOE Senior Education Manager): omissions were "not intentional"; the listening tour was "the first of many steps."

**Named critics:** **Glenn Branch** (NCSE): "I would like to say something nice about the new standards, but I can't" (Orlando Sentinel, Sept 30 2026, via [NCSE's republished commentary](https://ncse.ngo/proposed-new-florida-science-standards-attract-fierce-criticism), Oct 2 2026). **Bertha Vazquez** (former Miami MS science teacher; Director, Teacher Institute for Evolutionary Science; NCSE "Friend of Darwin"): "moving backward... If it's not broken, why the hell are we fixing it? We had smash bang good standards." **Robin Williams** (retired biology/environmental-science teacher): "Evolution is a fundamental underlying process unifying all of biology. To leave it out is ideology posing as biology." **Brad Dupke** (earth-science teacher): "We need to call a spade a spade instead of trying to dance around what it is." **Lindsey Craven** (Science Specialist, 9–12, Pinellas County): standards "reference... evolution without actually... stating evolution." **Charlotte Bernhardt** (biology teacher, Durant HS, Plant City): concern about FL students' college-admission/placement disadvantage. **Florida Education Association** (state teachers' union): warned students are left "at a disadvantage"; formally requested extended review and more virtual town halls. Parents/teachers at listening sessions described (Florida Phoenix) as "gobsmacked." **Grazie Pozo Christie**, a State Board of Education member, named/quoted in Florida Phoenix's board-level coverage (exact quote not captured — follow up directly).

**NCSE's institutional position:** clearly critical across three 2026 posts — [Oct 9](https://ncse.ngo/florida-opens-pandoras-box-educational-horrors-proposed-new-science-standards), [Oct 2](https://ncse.ngo/proposed-new-florida-science-standards-attract-fierce-criticism), [Aug 21 (2024 textbook removal)](https://ncse.ngo/how-florida-quietly-removed-climate-change-content-textbooks).

## Florida public-records / copyright status of state curriculum documents

Florida state-agency works, including FLDOE standards and rule text, are public records and not subject to copyright claims by the state, absent specific legislative authorization:
- **Florida Statutes §119.011(12)** and ch. 119 generally define "public record" expansively and require such records be open for inspection/copying.
- **Florida Constitution, Art. I, §24(a)** constitutionally guarantees public access to records of the legislative, executive, and judicial branches and their agencies.
- Case law: ***Microdecisions, Inc. v. Skinner***, 889 So.2d 871 (Fla. 2d DCA 2004) — text/data produced by Florida government in its official capacity is public domain; the state cannot assert copyright over it.
- Narrow legislatively-specified exceptions exist (e.g. Fla. Stat. §24.105(10), Dept. of Lottery; §601.101, Dept. of Citrus) — none apply to FLDOE curriculum standards.

Source: [Wikipedia, "Copyright status of works by the government of Florida"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_status_of_works_by_the_government_of_Florida) — a tertiary source, but its statute/case citations are specific enough to verify independently; pull §119.011 and the *Microdecisions* opinion directly before citing in a context that needs primary-source confirmation. The legal conclusion (FLDOE standards are public domain) is consistent with general US state-government-works doctrine.

**Practical implication for this repository:** verbatim standard/benchmark text (1996, 2008 NGSSS, and whatever is adopted in December 2026) can be quoted and archived in full without copyright concern — this repo's `accessTier`/`licenseOrRightsNote` fields can cite this directly for any FLDOE-sourced government text (not for third-party news coverage, which remains ordinary copyright + fair use).
