## What's already unblocked, no review needed

- Institutional-actor records (who NCERT/CBSE/state boards are, legally and structurally) — factual, non-text, already built (see `../../national_ccs_policy/` and `../../state/`).
- Karnataka-sourced content, up to and including full text, citing DSERT's confirmed **CC BY-SA 4.0** license on KOER directly — a real, already-open case, not a hypothetical.
- Short, clearly-bounded quotation of a body's own licensing/rights *notice* (not its curriculum content) for identification/commentary purposes — ordinary, low-risk fair-dealing practice already used in this repo's own timeline records.

## What genuinely needs a real legal read before going further

1. **The core unresolved question: does NCERT/CBSE's status as a registered society (not a literal government department) put their published works inside Section 2(k)/17(d)'s "government work" definition, or under ordinary copyright rules?** This isn't a rhetorical question — the research pass found real scholarly disagreement (one source treats it as settled in NCERT's favor as a government-work case; no court ruling was found either way) and it changes the practical redistribution risk meaningfully, since a "government work" carries a different (fixed 60-year) term than ordinary corporate/anonymous-work copyright.
2. **A direct, human-verified read of NCERT's actual current digital-content licenses** — epathshala.nic.in and osre.ncert.gov.in's license pages blocked every automated fetch attempt during the research pass. The unverified claim floating in search results (that some NCERT textbooks are CC BY-NC-ND on the DIKSHA platform) directly conflicts with NCERT's own all-rights-reserved language confirmed elsewhere. This needs someone to actually load those specific pages, not another search pass.
3. **State-by-state confirmation, prioritized by which state(s) this project actually wants to draw from first** — Karnataka and Maharashtra are now confirmed (opposite ends of the spectrum); every other state, including Tamil Nadu, remains unchecked. This mirrors exactly the per-Land discipline `deutsche_lp` already uses for Germany.
4. **A practical, not just theoretical, risk read.** The research pass found a real precedent shape worth a lawyer's specific comment on: Indian government/quasi-government bodies do send takedown-style legal notices over bulk republication (the BIS standards case), but public-interest pushback has previously produced voluntary partial opening rather than a court loss for the redistributor — and separately, informal bulk-hosting of NCERT text already exists on GitHub/Hugging Face with no confirmed enforcement action found either way. A lawyer should weigh in specifically on what OpenEvo's actual exposure looks like given that mixed picture, not just recite the statute.

## Who would actually do this — real options, not a single prescribed path

This is a genuine choice about how much to invest and how, not a technical call — worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting:

- **A formal opinion from an India-qualified IP/copyright lawyer.** The most defensible path if this project will eventually store substantial NCERT/CBSE/state curriculum text at scale. Real cost and lead time; the right move if India becomes a serious, ongoing part of this ecosystem's work rather than a single pilot.
- **A lower-cost, informal expert read first.** The research pass surfaced real, already-engaged scholarship on exactly this question — the SpicyIP pieces (Dhonchak, Bajaj) and the underlying 2021 *Indian Journal of Constitutional Law* article are written by people who have already thought hard about this specific issue. A direct outreach or consultation (even informal) with that circle could sharpen the open questions above considerably before committing to a full formal opinion.
- **Direct outreach to NCERT/CBSE/a specific state board for explicit permission**, the way Maharashtra's Balbharati already runs a real licensing process for third-party publishers, or the way German state ministries have granted explicit real partnerships elsewhere in this ecosystem's own work. Slower and not guaranteed, but would resolve the question outright for whichever source agrees, and sidesteps the "government work" ambiguity entirely by making it contractual instead.
- **Proceed conservatively without a formal review for now**, building only on already-confirmed-open content (Karnataka) and citation-only/short-excerpt records elsewhere, and revisit once India work grows past the exploratory stage. Lowest cost, but leaves the core national-level (NCERT/CBSE) question open indefinitely.

None of these are mutually exclusive — a real informal read now doesn't preclude a formal opinion later, and outreach to a specific state can proceed in parallel with either.

## What doesn't need re-litigating

The four gaps in the research doc's own "Gaps flagged" section (government-work status, NCERT's current digital licenses, Tamil Nadu, the BIS PIL's final disposition) are the actual open items — a real review's checklist should start there, not re-derive the landscape from scratch.
