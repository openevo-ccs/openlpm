**Subject: A quick question on Indian curriculum-document copyright, building on your 2021 IJCL piece**

Dear Mr. Bajaj,

I'm Dustin Eirdosh, with OpenEvo CCS Lab / GlobalESD [adjust this line to taste]. I came across your 2021 paper with Anupriya Dhonchak in the *Indian Journal of Constitutional Law* ("Government Copyright in School Textbooks and the Fundamental Right to Education") while researching whether and how we could responsibly reference Indian curriculum documents in our own work.

We're a small education-research project comparing how different countries and states structure school curricula, often around topics like human evolution and biology, and we want to handle Indian sources carefully rather than guess. Your paper is the most specific, well-reasoned treatment we've found of exactly the question we're stuck on: whether NCERT and state textbook boards' output counts as a "government work" under Sections 2(k)/17(d)/17(dd) of the Copyright Act, given their status as registered societies rather than literal government departments.

Two questions, if you have a few minutes:

1. Four years on, does your 2021 reading still hold up in your view, and has anything — a ruling, a policy change — actually settled it since?
2. For a project like ours, storing and discussing short excerpts for educational/research purposes rather than republishing full texts commercially, how would you size the practical risk against the stricter statutory reading?

We're not asking for a formal legal opinion, just hoping to benefit from the thinking you've already done before we settle on an approach. Happy to share more about the project if that's useful.

Thank you for your time either way,
Dustin Eirdosh
[contact / affiliation line]

## What this does and doesn't unblock
