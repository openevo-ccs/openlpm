# state/kerala

A restrictive case, closer in practical effect to [`../maharashtra/`](../maharashtra/)
than to [`../karnataka/`](../karnataka/), but a different flavor of
restrictive: Kerala's own textbooks (published by SCERT, an autonomous
body of the Department of General Education, Government of Kerala) carry a
bare copyright notice with no stated reuse license anywhere checked —
unlike Maharashtra's Balbharati, no fee-based licensing regime or publisher
dispute was found. The copyright holder named on the textbook itself is a
literal government department, not a registered society — a cleaner
"government work" case under Copyright Act 1957 Section 2(k) than NCERT's
own situation, though it works the same direction (restrictive), not the
opposite one.

Added 2026-10-02, after a partner working in Kerala asked about covering
the Kerala Curriculum Framework (KCF). What's here reflects what's
actually safe given Kerala's confirmed licensing posture: real,
page-cited structural detail (what a chapter covers, where, and how it
compares to national content) rather than quoted text.

The one genuinely distinctive finding: Kerala has a real, on-the-record
public dispute with NCERT — not a licensing dispute, a content one — over
NCERT's 2023-2024 deletion of evolution/Darwin content from national
textbooks, which Kerala's own Chief Minister publicly criticized, with
SCERT directing schools to keep teaching the deleted material. See
[`local_policy/records/coherence-findings.yaml`](local_policy/records/coherence-findings.yaml)
for the real chapter-level detail behind that.

**Read [`../../GOVERNANCE.md`](../../GOVERNANCE.md) before treating
anything here as settled fact for a state beyond Karnataka, Maharashtra,
and now Kerala** — most Indian states remain unresearched.

## 2026-10-09: forest-dwelling tribal communities, residential schooling, and offline access

Added toward a planned outside anthropological review of this content --
the first outside review this repository is being built toward -- and the
broader question of what a model Learning Progression Map for Kerala's diverse
communities would need to look like. Covers Kerala's forest-dwelling
Scheduled Tribe communities whose children board away from family at
residential schools (Model Residential Schools, Ashram Schools, Eklavya
Model Residential Schools, pre-metric hostels), Kerala's own offline/
low-connectivity digital-education reality (KITE, Samagra, First Bell, and
documented hamlet-level electricity/connectivity gaps that are more severe
than "has a phone but no signal"), real named bridge-language programs
(KIRTADS' Paniya primers, "Gothrabandhu" mentor-teachers, the Muthuvan-
specific "Padippurasi" workshop in Edamalakudy, and a COVID-era
mother-tongue video program reported under two different, unreconciled
names), and a direct, primary-source read of KCF 2023 itself.

**The single most consequential finding**: KCF 2023 explicitly names Scheduled
Tribe children as needing "special care and support" and mandates a real
governance structure (Tribal Education Committees) for them, but its
English text names no specific tribal community, language, or district,
and its otherwise well-developed mother-tongue pedagogy is never extended
to a tribal language. See
[`local_policy/records/coherence-findings.yaml`](local_policy/records/coherence-findings.yaml)'s
`INLP-FINDING-kcf2023-names-tribal-equity-but-omits-tribal-specifics` for
the full analysis, and
[`ccs_policy/records/policy-principles.yaml`](ccs_policy/records/policy-principles.yaml)
for what this repo is proposing to do about that gap.

**Honesty check, stated plainly**: this pass was built entirely from
published government, news, and academic sources read or searched by an
AI research session -- real and carefully, individually sourced (each
record's own `contributor_notes` says exactly what was directly read
versus what rests on a search-engine summary still needing confirmation),
but not yet reviewed by the planned outside anthropological reviewer, by
anyone with direct knowledge of these specific communities, or by anyone
able to read the Malayalam
edition of KCF. Several specific figures (hostel/school counts, dropout
percentages, dialect counts for the COVID-era bridge programs) are
flagged inline as inconsistent across sources and not yet resolved.
Nothing here should be presented to an outside audience as settled until
that review happens.
