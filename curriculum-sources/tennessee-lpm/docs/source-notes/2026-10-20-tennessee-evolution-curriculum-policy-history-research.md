# Tennessee evolution-curriculum policy history — founding research (2026-10-20)

Full primary-source research on Tennessee's evolution-education policy history, 1925-2026, organized by era. Every entry has date, description, named actors, and source. Items confirmed only via a secondary summary, or where sources genuinely conflict, are flagged explicitly.

## Era 1: The Butler Act and the Scopes Trial (1925-1927)

### The Butler Act

Tennessee House Bill No. 185, introduced by **Rep. John Washington Butler** on **1925-01-21**. House passed **71-5** on **1925-01-28**. Senate passed **24-6** on **1925-03-13**. Signed by **Governor Austin Peay** on **1925-03-21**.

**Exact statutory text (Section 1)**: "Be it enacted by the General Assembly of the State of Tennessee, That it shall be unlawful for any teacher in any of the Universities, Normals and all other public schools of the State which are supported in whole or in part by the public school funds of the State, to teach any theory that denies the story of the Divine Creation of man as taught in the Bible, and to teach instead that man has descended from a lower order of animals." Penalty (Section 2): misdemeanor, fine of **not less than $100 nor more than $500 per offense**.

Peay signed it to shore up support among rural legislators, said "nothing of consequence in the books now being taught in our schools" would run afoul of it, stated "The people must have the right to regulate what is taught in their schools," and said he did not believe it would ever actually be enforced. Sources: [Butler Act — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler_Act); [Butler Act full text](https://todayinsci.com/S/Scopes_John/ButlerAct.htm); [Austin Peay — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Peay).

### The Scopes Trial (State of Tennessee v. John Thomas Scopes, 1925)

The ACLU placed a newspaper ad (reported 1925-05-04, Chattanooga Times) offering to defend any Tennessee teacher willing to test the Butler Act. **George Rappleyea**, a Dayton civic booster, convinced Dayton businessmen at Robinson's Drug Store to recruit **John T. Scopes**, a local substitute teacher/football coach, as test defendant — explicitly to generate publicity and business for the town.

**Trial dates**: **1925-07-10 through 1925-07-21**, Rhea County Courthouse, Dayton, Tennessee. **Judge**: John T. Raulston — a conservative fundamentalist who opened proceedings with prayer (over Darrow's objection) and was openly partial to the prosecution. **Prosecution**: District Attorney Tom Stewart, local attorneys Herbert E. Hicks and Sue K. Hicks, Gordon McKenzie, joined by **William Jennings Bryan** (three-time Democratic presidential candidate, former Secretary of State under Wilson). **Defense**: **Clarence Darrow** (lead), Arthur Garfield Hays (ACLU), Dudley Field Malone, John R. Neal, W.O. Thompson, F.B. McElwee, plus biblical-authority witness Charles Francis Potter.

On day 7, with the jury excused, Darrow called Bryan himself as a witness to question his literal reading of the Bible. Asked where Cain found his wife, Bryan replied he'd "leave the agnostics to hunt for her." The exchange ran roughly two hours before Raulston adjourned court; generally treated as having damaged Bryan's credibility even though Raulston struck the testimony from the record the next day.

**Verdict**: Scopes found guilty, fined **$100**, 1925-07-21 — the fine set by Judge Raulston, not the jury (the basis of the 1927 reversal). **Media circus**: H.L. Mencken (Baltimore Sun) coined "Monkey Trial," mocked both sides (left before Darrow's cross-examination of Bryan). First American trial broadcast live nationally by radio (Chicago's WGN, announcer Quin Ryan). Sources: [Scopes trial — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scopes_trial); [George Rappleyea — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Rappleyea); [Science History Institute](https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/disappearing-pod/the-scopes-monkey-trial-publicity-stunt/).

### The appeal — Scopes v. State, Tennessee Supreme Court

**Citation**: 289 S.W. 363, 154 Tenn. 105 (Tenn. 1927). Date: January 1927 (exact day disputed across sources — treat as unconfirmed at the day level). 3-1 decision, Chief Justice **Grafton Green** writing: the Butler Act was held **constitutional**, but Scopes's conviction was **reversed** on the technical ground that Tennessee law required fines over $50 be set by a jury, not a judge — the judge-imposed $100 fine was improper. The court's own words: "**Nothing is to be gained by prolonging the life of this bizarre case**." Green recommended the state enter a *nolle prosequi* rather than retry Scopes, to preserve "the peace and dignity of the state" — deliberately shutting the prosecution down without striking the law itself. The state did not retry Scopes. Sources: [Scopes trial — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scopes_trial); [Americans United, "Scopes forever"](https://www.au.org/the-latest/articles/scopes-forever-the-long-reach-of-a-famous-trial/); [TIME archive, "Education: Bizarre"](https://time.com/archive/6777617/education-bizarre/).

### Bryan's death

**1925-07-26** — five days after the trial concluded, in Dayton, Tennessee, in his sleep (reported stroke) after church and an afternoon nap. Mencken privately said "We killed the son-of-a-bitch!" and joked "God aimed at Darrow, missed, and hit Bryan instead." His published obituary (Baltimore Evening Sun, 1925-07-27) was openly hostile. Sources: [William Jennings Bryan — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jennings_Bryan); [NCSE, "Stop Press! Mencken's Obituary for Bryan"](https://ncse.ngo/stop-press-menckens-obituary-bryan).

## Era 2: Repeal (1967) and the "equal time" law (1973-1975)

### Butler Act repeal, 1967

**Gary L. Scott**, a science teacher in Jacksboro, Tennessee, was fired **1967-04-13** for teaching evolution — unlike Scopes, not a staged test case; Scott said he didn't realize he'd broken the law. He sued for reinstatement on First Amendment grounds with ACLU help, represented by **William Kunstler** (later the Chicago Seven's attorney). Within three days of his filing suit, a repeal bill (House Bill No. 48, Chapter No. 237) passed both chambers, **1967-05-13**. Signed by **Governor Buford Ellington** (date disputed between 1967-05-17 and 1967-05-18 across sources; 05-18 better supported). Effective **1967-09-01**.

**Relationship to Epperson v. Arkansas**: Tennessee's repeal came roughly 18 months BEFORE the US Supreme Court's *Epperson v. Arkansas* ruling (decided 1968-11-12), which struck down Arkansas's near-identical law. Tennessee's repeal was NOT a response to Epperson — driven independently by the Gary Scott controversy, predating the Supreme Court ruling. Scott was reinstated with back pay even before the repeal was finalized. Sources: [Butler Act — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler_Act); [Knox TN Today, "His name was Gary Scott, and he is a hero"](https://www.knoxtntoday.com/his-name-was-gary-scott-and-he-is-a-hero/); [Scientific American, "50 Years Ago: Repeal of Tennessee's Monkey Law"](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/observations/50-years-ago-repeal-of-tennessees-monkey-law/).

### The 1973 "equal time" / "Genesis Bill"

**Dr. Russell Artist**, a biology professor at David Lipscomb College, pushed the idea through the late 1960s/early 1970s, approaching fellow Church of Christ member **Sen. Milton H. Hamilton** (Union City) to sponsor it. A House companion had backing from House Speaker Ned McWherter and Rep. Tommy Bennett (Jamestown). **Rep. Bill Carter** (Rhea County — home of the original Scopes trial) championed it explicitly as continuing the earlier fight: "Since I unsuccessfully opposed the repeal of the 'Evolution Law'... I have been duty bound to do all I can to see that this bill is passed." Bill numbers: SB 394 / HB 597, enacted as Chapter 377 of the 1973 Public Acts, nicknamed the "Theory and Fact Law" or "Genesis Bill." Senate floor debate began 1973-04-18; House/Senate votes 1973-04-26 through 1973-04-30; final passage **1973-04-30**. **Governor Winfield Dunn** signed it, reportedly avoiding public comment.

**Statutory text**: required any biology textbook expressing an opinion on "origins or creation of man and his world" give "commensurate attention to, and an equal amount of emphasis on" other theories, "including, but not limited to, the Genesis account in the Bible." Textbooks discussing evolution had to state it was "not represented to be scientific fact" — a disclaimer not applied to the Bible, which the statute defined as a "reference work." Scheduled effective 1975. Sources: [Journal of Southern Religion, "'Equal Space With Adam and Eve': Tennessee's 'Genesis Bill' of 1973"](https://jsreligion.org/issues/vol16/edwards.html); [Daniel v. Waters — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_v._Waters).

### Daniel v. Waters (1975)

Plaintiffs: Tennessee biology teachers and parents, joined by the **National Association of Biology Teachers**, against the state textbook board. Federal district court (M.D. Tenn.) initially abstained on the merits (1974-02-26); on remand issued a final ruling, 399 F. Supp. 510 (M.D. Tenn. 1975), finding the law gave "a clearly defined preferential position for the Biblical version of creation," amounting to "the very establishment of religion which the First Amendment... squarely forbids." **Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals**, decided **1975-04-10**, 515 F.2d 485 (6th Cir. 1975) — **Judge George Edwards** wrote the majority opinion, calling the law "patently unconstitutional" on two grounds: special treatment of "occult or satanical beliefs," and special treatment of the Genesis account specifically. Not appealed further. Source: [Daniel v. Waters — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_v._Waters); [TalkOrigins case summary](http://www.talkorigins.org/faqs/daniel-v-waters.html).

## Era 3: The 2012 "Monkey Law" (Tennessee Teacher Protection Act)

### HB 368 / SB 893 (2011-2012 session)

Sponsors: **Rep. Bill Dunn** (House), **Sen. Bo Watson** (R-Hixson) (Senate). HB 368 passed House **70-23** on **2011-04-07**. SB 893 stalled in Senate committee through 2011, revived 2012-03-07, cleared Senate Education Committee **7-1** on 2012-03-14 (amended "may cause debate and disputation" for "can cause controversy"), passed full Senate **24-8** on **2012-03-20**. Transmitted to Governor Bill Haslam 2012-03-29.

**What it does**: bars the State Board of Education and any school administrator from stopping a teacher from helping students "understand, analyze, critique, and review in an objective manner the scientific strengths and scientific weaknesses of existing scientific theories" on subjects that "cause debate and disputation" — naming **biological evolution, the chemical origins of life, global warming (climate change), and human cloning** — while directing schools to foster scientific inquiry and critical thinking generally.

**Governor Haslam's handling**: let it become law **without his signature** on **2012-04-10** — the first bill of his term he'd done this to. Stated reasoning: he did not believe the law changed the science curriculum or taught anything not already allowed, but "good legislation should bring clarity and not confusion. My concern is that this bill has not met this objective." Effective date disputed across sources (April 20, 2012 vs. July 1, 2012); codified at **T.C.A. § 49-6-1030** (Acts 2012, ch. 670, §2). Tennessee was the **second state** to pass this type of law, after Louisiana's 2008 Louisiana Science Education Act.

**Opposition**: NCSE (executive director Eugenie C. Scott: "Telling students that evolution and climate change are scientifically controversial is miseducating them"); Tennessee Science Teachers Association president **Becky Ashe** ("unnecessary, anti-scientific, and very likely unconstitutional," warning it let teachers "teach a Sunday school class" in science class); also AAAS, ACLU of Tennessee, American Institute for Biological Sciences, National Association of Biology Teachers, National Association of Geoscience Teachers, National Earth Science Teachers Association, Tennessee's National Academy of Sciences members, and the Knoxville News Sentinel/Nashville Tennessean editorial boards. **Support**: Sen. Watson framed it as promoting critical thinking, not an attack on evolution. Sources: [NCSE, "Background on Tennessee's 21st Century Monkey Law"](https://ncse.ngo/background-tennessees-21st-century-monkey-law); [NCSE, "Tennessee 'monkey bill' passes legislature"](https://ncse.ngo/tennessee-monkey-bill-passes-legislature); [NCSE, "'Monkey bill' enacted in Tennessee"](https://ncse.ngo/monkey-bill-enacted-tennessee); [Times Free Press, 2012-03-20](https://www.timesfreepress.com/news/news/story/2012/mar/20/tn-senate-passes-watsons-bill-on-evolution-debate/73438/); [HuffPost, 2012-04-10](https://www.huffingtonpost.com/2012/04/10/tennessee-evolution-bill-haslam_n_1416015.html).

### Current status (2012-2026)

Still in force, unchanged since 2012 — no evidence found of amendment or repeal. **No evidence found this law has ever been directly challenged in court or invoked in a documented case** (absence-of-evidence, not confirmed negative — search was not exhaustive). No comparable 2020s Tennessee evolution-standards controversy found (contrast Florida's 2026 fight) — the main recent developments are the 2022 standards revision (below) and 100th-anniversary-of-Scopes retrospective coverage in 2025. Source: [Scopes Trial Centennial, "Tennessee Passed A New 'Monkey Bill' In 2012. It's Still In Force"](https://scopestrialcentennial.com/tennessee-passed-a-new-monkey-bill-in-2012-its-still-in-force/); [WBUR On Point, 2025-07-28](https://www.wbur.org/onpoint/2025/07/28/scopes-monkey-trial-100-years).

## Tennessee's current state science standards

**Tennessee Academic Standards for Science**, adopted **2022-10-28** by the **Tennessee State Board of Education**, replacing the 2016 standards, rolling into classrooms for the **2025-26 school year**. Statutory authority: T.C.A. § 49-1-302(a)(8); review process mandated at least every 8 years by T.C.A. §§ 49-1-311 to 313 / Board Policy 3.209 — educator review teams draft, a public-comment period runs (over 10,000 comments in the 2022 cycle), teams revise, the State Board casts a final adoption vote. Evolution content: a dedicated strand, **LS4: Biological Change: Unity and Diversity**, covering Natural Selection, Adaptation, and Biodiversity and Humans; expanded grade-7 focus on natural selection/adaptation/biodiversity; expanded climate/environmental content.

**Important correction to a plausible assumption**: a widely cited "Tennessee gets a D" finding comes from the **2009** NCSE study (Mead & Mates), evaluating the PRIOR generation of standards — no NCSE or comparable evaluation of the CURRENT 2022 standards was found. Don't treat the "D" grade as describing today's standards. Sources: [TN State Board of Education, Jan 2022](https://www.tn.gov/sbe/news/2022/1/31/science-standards.html); [2022-10-28 adoption PDF](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/stateboardofeducation/documents/standards/science/New%2010-28-22%20Science%20Standards.pdf); [NCSE, "Evolution: How Does It Fare in State K-12 Science Standards?"](https://ncse.ngo/evolution-how-does-it-fare-state-k-12-science-standards).

## Tennessee's public-records/copyright stance — more complicated than a clean binary

Raw statutory text is not copyrightable under the federal government-edicts doctrine (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, Inc.*, 2020). But a real, active Tennessee dispute exists about ACCESS, not copyright per se: **Carl Malamud's Public.Resource.Org**, with Nashville law professor David Hudson, wrote Tennessee's Revisor of Statutes (letter dated 2021-04-14) asking the state confirm the Tennessee Code Annotated is public domain. When unresolved, Public.Resource.Org sued **LexisNexis** (Matthew Bender & Company) in Davidson County Chancery Court (filed ~late July 2022), arguing LexisNexis — which holds the state's contract to compile/sell the official annotated code — is "the functional equivalent of state government" and must provide full public-records access. The **Tennessee Court of Appeals ruled 2023-11-09** (*Public.Resource.Org v. Matthew Bender & Company*, M2022-01260-COA-R3-CV) that LexisNexis is a private contractor, not a state actor, not bound by public-records law. **Net effect**: Tennessee doesn't formally claim copyright over statute text, but the complete official annotated code remains gated behind a commercial product, upheld by the courts — a third distinct posture from both Florida (freely public) and Pennsylvania (direct state copyright claim, unexercised on standards docs). Sources: [Nashville Post](https://www.nashvillepost.com/business/legal/lexisnexis-sued-over-access-to-tennessee-code-annotated/article_240b01e2-0f6c-11ed-8a39-2f0d5302de2c.html); [Tennessee Court of Appeals opinion, Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/tennessee/court-of-appeals/2023/m2022-01260-coa-r3-cv.html).
